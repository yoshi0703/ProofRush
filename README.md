# ProofRush

## Instance-optimal identity testing at the specified distance

For every finite or countable discrete reference law, we give a functional of the law and the requested total variation distance that characterizes the minimum sample count within universal factors. For finite real inputs the formula uses prefix sums and fixed powers. For explicitly listed rational masses and binary multiplicities, a rounded version runs in polynomial bit time. This is our proposed answer to Clément Canonne’s COLT 2024 Open Question 1. External specialist review and prize adjudication are pending. [English preprint](studies/canonne-q1/paper/paper.pdf) · [Proof, evaluator and independent verification](studies/canonne-q1/README.md)

## Exact-radius identity testing

Two preprints study how many observations distinguish a known discrete distribution from alternatives at a specified total variation distance. The first gives matching bounds at every strict radius for a fixed number of diffuse bins and constructs references that share entropy, maximum atom and any prescribed finite list of power sums while their testing costs diverge in ratio. The second determines the worst-case order over reference distributions, including a regime near maximum distance where one observation suffices. The latter corrects the all-parameter lower-bound claim in ICALP 2018. Both manuscripts await external review. The subsequent instance-specific theorem is described above. [Fixed-bin paper](studies/exact-radius-identity/diffuse-bins.pdf) · [Worst-case paper](studies/exact-radius-identity/worst-case.pdf) · [Proof scope, exact code and reproduction](studies/exact-radius-identity/README.md)

## Can a fixed number of square boundaries cover a grid graph?

Takumi Shiota posed this problem at CCCG 2025. We prove NP-completeness by constructing a unit grid graph whose minimum square-boundary cover equals the vertex-cover number of the source graph plus an explicitly computable offset. The construction handles exactly $k$ placements with or without repetition. The preprint and proof await external specialist review. [Original question](https://cccg-wads-2025.eecs.yorku.ca/CCCG_2025_Open_Problems.pdf#page=3) · [English preprint](studies/shiota-square-cover/paper/paper.pdf) · [Construction, Lean proof and replay](studies/shiota-square-cover/README.md)

## A direct comparison for instance-optimal identity testing

Clément Canonne asked in 2019 how two quantities used to bound the number of observations needed to test a known distribution are related. He returned to the question at COLT 2024. We prove both sides of his displayed inequality directly for every finite or countably supported probability law. The sharper lower bound holds at the same error tolerance. The upper bound uses two tolerances. We prove the optimal multiplier of the reciprocal tolerance gap is one quarter and construct distributions that require a change of tolerance. The source inequality and the stronger two-sided comparison have been checked in Lean and independently in Rust. The paper awaits external review. The study above addresses Canonne's separate AUD 200 question on sample complexity. ## Spelling identity in byte-pair encoding

Baliga, Xie and Byju asked whether their greedy BPE guarantee extends to ambiguous initial spellings. A 14-token example refutes it. When the number of initial token types is unrestricted, the worst-case ratio is of order \(1/\sqrt{k}\) at merge budget \(k\), even with the best tie order. With at most a fixed number of initial token types, the ratio tends uniformly to one, including for ambiguous spellings. Both results apply to disjoint replacement counts and raw overlapping counts and have complete independent Lean/Rust checks. An explicit 338-merge example saves 4,732 tokens under every greedy run, while one feasible comparator saves 30,758. [English paper](studies/string-identified-bpe/paper.pdf) · [Rate statements](studies/string-identified-bpe/lean/Approximation.lean) · [Independent verification](studies/string-identified-bpe/VERIFY.md)

Starting from the 64 individual ASCII letters underlying the short counterexample, the four mathematical greedy merges save 60 tokens and attain the optimum. Starting from the fourteen prescribed tokens and counting overlapping observations, all legal greedy histories save five against an optimum of ten. Both initialization and pair ranking affect what transfers to a trainer. [Scope and two native tokenizer replays](studies/string-identified-bpe/SCOPE.md) · [Source authors’ open question](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08671v3#S8) · [Research chronology](studies/string-identified-bpe/RESEARCH.md)

## Value dimension in one softmax attention layer

Hsu and Xu asked how much value dimension is needed to classify symmetric Boolean functions with one fixed-query attention layer. With \(H\) heads and readout degree \(D\), let \(r\) be the least positive integer with \(\binom{D+r}{r}\ge HD+1\). The least universal dimension satisfies \(\min(D,r)/3\le d_*(H,D)\le\min(D,32r)\). In particular, when \(D\ge3\) and \(H\ge4^D\), some target requires at least \(D-1\) channels, and \(D\) always suffice. The joint theorem has a complete Lean proof on bit vectors, independently replayed in the Rust kernel nanoda. External peer review is pending. ## Masked discrete diffusion

A two-bit distribution disproves the support-ordering conjecture of He, Rojas and Tao (ICLR 2026, Conjecture E.1) and the limiting-support conclusion of Proposition 3.3(2). We also prove a finite-alphabet theorem identifying which outputs survive strong guidance, a subset recurrence computing their decay exponents and coefficients, and a product construction in which arbitrarily rare class overlap becomes nearly certain. The English manuscript and formal proofs are available for independent review. External peer review is pending. ## When partial beach counts reverse the direction of change

Five of 17 consecutive daily changes at two South African beaches reversed sign when counts were restricted to the upper zone. At Spina in Italy, a seasonal full-beach increase of 74 objects accompanied an inland decrease of at least 41. The study also evaluates a random bag-counting design for estimating site-period totals while retaining full-width clearance. The note uses archived field observations and awaits external peer review. ## How transect selection affects shoreline litter surveys

One spreadsheet option in NOAA's 2021 field guide gives each end strip of a beach a 12.92% or 13.30% chance of selection under two ways of redrawing duplicates. Uniform selection gives 20%. A single four-strip visit without a record of the selection procedure cannot yield an estimator unbiased for all count patterns under all three designs. In two disjoint samples of eligible US sites, the site-weighted frequencies of observing an end strip were 17.2% and 12.6%. The public records identify neither the drawing procedure nor how much litter selection may have missed. This exploratory methods report has not undergone external peer review. ## Forced outcomes in Althöfer's alternating Collatz game

We classify exactly the starts where the second player can force a win on the second move. Four infinite families suffice, beginning at 7, 29, 57 and 227. Two inverse constructions propagate finite losing strategies. They generate a self-similar set of 2-adic limits, including limits of losses in every prescribed residue class modulo a power of three. The dyadic closures of both the forced wins and the forced losses within each such class have Hausdorff dimension at least 0.57969. Althöfer's €500 problem of proving finite optimal play from every odd start remains open. This preprint awaits external peer review. ## Hankel matrices in Shallit's four-integer sequence

Jeffrey Shallit asked whether every shifted Hankel determinant of a particular four-integer sequence is nonzero. We prove that at every starting position and above every positive order R there is a nonsingular order at most 13R. Lean and the independent Rust kernel checked this result. A separate mathematical proof with two exact implementations shows that the rational rank of every order-n matrix is greater than 13n/43. All shifts are nonsingular through order 4,096. The original all-orders question remains open, and the work awaits external peer review. ### A cubic counterexample to AutoGraphForge conjecture 5ed263d5

A connected 14-vertex graph with degree three at every vertex has diameter **8** and vertex clique cover number **6**. Pastorek's 2026 candidate would require its diameter to be at most **7**. The witness disproves the universal inequality. [Original candidate, line 2530](https://github.com/JanPastorek/AutoGraphForge/blob/4d1a8afc99447cacd2328cca20e47b6493e8dbc5/results/survivors_by_touch_normalised.txt#L2530) · [Catalogue paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.03478)

The graph already occurs in the 1976 cubic census and in the 2007 family of Brand, Guiduli and Imrich. The result reported here is its application to the later conjecture. Two bridges force two specified vertices to lie eight edges apart. Six cliques cover the vertices, and a six-vertex independent set proves that any cover needs at least that many cliques. The English note includes a labeled diagram and the complete proof. [1976 census](https://pure.tue.nl/ws/files/1713766/252909.pdf) · [2007 construction](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/students/guiduli.dir/barry-brand-imrich.pdf) · [Paper PDF](paper/paper.pdf) · [LaTeX source](paper/paper.tex)

## Verification and priority

The negative answer can also be deduced directly from the older diameter result. A diameter path in the known 42-vertex graph has 15 disjoint edges. Those two-vertex cliques and the 12 remaining singletons show that its clique cover number is at most 27, although its diameter is 29. Our formal certificate works on the smaller 14-vertex graph and computes both invariants exactly. [Brand, Guiduli and Imrich](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/students/guiduli.dir/barry-brand-imrich.pdf)

Lean 4.36.0 and pinned Mathlib verified both the printed conjecture's negation and its negation on connected graphs, the domain where the source evaluates diameter. An independently built Rust kernel, nanoda, checked all **7,184** exported declarations. The only axioms were the three standard Lean foundations. A separate Python implementation recovered the same diameter and the optimum clique partition.
