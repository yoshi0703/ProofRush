# Sources and scope of the mathematical survey

## New mathematical results considered

- **Matrix multiplication.** The 24 November OpenAI release reports the square exponent 9/4 over every field. Its formalized square result concerns division-free arithmetic circuits computing formal polynomial identities. This matters when importing the exponent into work stated in another computational model. For example, the symbolic sparse-triangle exponent 2ω/(1+ω) becomes 18/13 at ω=9/4. Treating that substitution as a new algorithmic theorem would additionally require compatible models and uniformity. It has no role in the proof of the graph counterexample. [Release and manuscript](https://github.com/openai/math/tree/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/preprints/The-matrix-multiplication-exponent-is-nine-fourths-November-24-2026) · [Formal scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/lean/docs/107.md) · [Sparse-triangle bounds](https://people.csail.mit.edu/virgi/6.890/papers/cycles-ayz.pdf)
- **Factoring and discrete logarithms.** The 25 November Cogentic collection reports uniform Las Vegas integer factorization in expected time L_N[1/3,105], a separate deterministic divisor algorithm with a worst-case bound O(exp(3(log N)^(3/4))), and a discrete logarithm algorithm for fixed-characteristic finite fields under specified parameter conditions. The algorithms could accelerate searches for finite arithmetic witnesses. Their complexity bounds give no density or infinitude theorem about primes. This distinction affected the priority of factorization and prime-pattern candidates. [Las Vegas factoring](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc7/cogentic_research_papers/integer_factorization) · [Deterministic factoring](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc7/cogentic_research_papers/deterministic_factoring_l34) · [Discrete logarithms](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc7/cogentic_research_papers/discrete_logarithm)
- **Log-rank.** The cited manuscript improves the exponent in an upper bound for deterministic two-party communication complexity to 1/2−0.000005 for Boolean matrices of real rank at least two. A clique coloring of a complement and a monochromatic rectangle partition are different combinatorial objects. No reduction preserving the requisite quantities was obtained. [Log-rank paper](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc7/cogentic_research_papers/logrank_conjecture)
- **Edge reconstruction.** The density condition m≥1000n for the new edge-reconstruction theorem fails on a cubic graph, where m=3n/2. The theorem could not settle the selected candidate. [Edge-reconstruction paper](https://github.com/google-research/google-research/tree/4a7bc8ab0bc7/cogentic_research_papers/edge_reconstruction)
- **Barker sequences.** A candidate labeled open in Formal Conjectures became ineligible when the OpenAI circulant Hadamard release was checked, including its Barker-sequence consequences. This was a concrete case in which a later result changed the selection decision. [OpenAI result family and current scope](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/CONTENTS.md#family-179)

The named theorems were examined at the level of their primary claims and published formalization scope. This project did not independently replay their proofs. The released graph theorem depends only on the graph definitions, the Lean foundations and the import closure identified in its audit.

## Proof search and public problem sources

- The Cogentic paper's Section 2 assigns separate tasks to research, verification and retention of progress. Section 3 reports outcomes for five specified economic and online-learning problems. Appendix A supplies task-specific prompts, for example the time horizon and regret target in online inverse linear optimization. We used its practice of making the conjecture and success condition explicit and retaining failed approaches. [Cogentic paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40324v2) · [Actual prompts](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.40324v2#A1) · [Official site](https://sites.google.com/view/cogentic)
- The formal proof search study separates informal mathematical work from verification of Lean artifacts. We applied this distinction to statement alignment and the exported theorem. [Advancing Mathematics Research with AI-Driven Formal Proof Search](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.22763)
- Formal Conjectures supplied definitions and candidates in games, sequences, number theory and graph burning. LeanOpenProblems supplied examples of separately packaged problem statements and checking scripts. Both informed the decision to keep the graph proposition in its own module. [Formal Conjectures snapshot](https://github.com/google-deepmind/formal-conjectures/tree/ea8e3e198ded6fc73efb7f5653b74b28ac4ae1a8) · [LeanOpenProblems snapshot](https://github.com/epoch-research/LeanOpenProblems/tree/6d71225ff2a3793a48d85b46c8c329d6046c473d)
- Searches covered the Erdős Problems site and daily board, OEIS and its public data, arXiv, MathOverflow, ECCC and the AI mathematics catalogue. Proposal labels are discovery aids. The dated publications and pinned source rows in the [candidate register](../research-logs/candidates.md) and [priority audit](../research-logs/priority.md) give the evidence for the individual decisions.
