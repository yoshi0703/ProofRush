## Propagation and boundary review

For a first dependent Hankel column of order d, its monic relation holds down all n rows. The leading d×d minor must be nonsingular: a relation on those first d entries propagates through all n rows using the monic column recurrence. If a residual first fails at t≥n, the consecutive shifted residual vectors have distinct first nonzero coordinates below row d, and are independent of the first d columns. This gives r≥2n−1−t, hence a recurrence interval of length ≥2n−1−r+d. If the constant coefficient vanishes, removing the leading zero coefficients reduces the order and shifts the interval while preserving the number of equations. At least n equations remain, so the reduced order cannot be zero when every integer symbol is nonzero.

At scale B=2^j with j≥2, the four image codewords are linearly independent over Q. A reversible scalar recurrence of order D≤uB makes the adjacent expanded block(s) linear functions of u complete preceding (and, for the backward statement, following) expanded blocks. The indicator rows of u blocks satisfy u−1 independent equal-block-sum constraints, so their dimension is at most 3u+1. Injectivity of blockwise expansion preserves their row rank over Q; a modular witness above this dimension rules out the proposed recurrence. Forward/backward unequal-successor witnesses rule it out directly. For D≥81 the chosen B is ≥4, u=ceil(D/B) is in 21,...,40, and (u−1)B<D. The alignment margin (K(u)+1)B−1 is sufficient even when B−1 symbols precede the first full block. These inequalities support the printed strict final bound. For D≤80, order D+1≤81 and the reduced interval has ≥2D+1 symbols, so its leading (D+1)-square minor would contradict the verified small-order certificate.

## Qualitative prior art

The caution that **some** uniform linear rank lower bound already follows from known results is justified. σ³ of each of the four letters contains all four symbols, so the substitution is primitive. The actual word is overlap-free by the separate Lean theorem `ShallitWord.overlap_free`; hence it is aperiodic. Durand's [notes, Proposition 9](https://cantorsalta2015.sciencesconf.org/conference/cantorsalta2015/Durand.pdf) explicitly state that primitive substitutive sequences are linearly recurrent. Aperiodicity yields a right-special length-d factor; linear recurrence places both of its extensions inside every O(d)-length interval, so no fixed order-d deterministic recurrence can hold on such an interval. A first dependent column gives a recurrence of order d≤r over n rows, forcing r=Ω(n) uniformly over shifts. This deduction supplies no checked explicit 13/43 constant. [Mérai–Winterhof](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.10764) directly study linear complexity of automatic sequences over finite fields; that result should not be represented as the identical rational all-shifts theorem without checking hypotheses. This focused review does not establish originality of the stronger constant.
