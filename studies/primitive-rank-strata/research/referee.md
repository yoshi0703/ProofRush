# Internal referee report on the exact prime-difference strata

27 November 2026. Manuscript reviewed after revisions. This was an internal AI mathematical review. The authors incorporated source corrections and an independent calculation communicated during the review. External specialist review remains pending.

**Verdict. I would count this as one complete negative solution of a conjecture explicitly posed by a human researcher.** Kászonyi asks on printed p. 328 whether every intermediate exact primitive stratum is stratified semilinear. The instance $n=105$, $\alpha=2$ refutes the universal statement. The general proof establishes failure at every admissible intermediate rank and corrects the proper rank-one clause of Theorem 6. The classification of the context-free sublanguages gives the result value as a focused research note. The all-slices conjecture and the full primitive-word language remain separate open questions. In particular, $Q_{105}$ was already known to be context-free. [Kászonyi, pp. 325–329](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1506/download/10_5486_PMD_2010_4680.pdf#page=13) · [Kászonyi and Katsura, 1997](https://doi.org/10.5486/PMD.1997.1707)

## Agreement with the original question

The source encodes each word with exactly one gap per marked letter and expressly allows a gap of zero. Definition 4 mislabels the prime that must be coprime to a scale numerator. Lemma 2 uses invertibility modulo the selected denominator. Definition 6’s empty-set symbol in a numerical test is resolved by the zero comparison used later in the paper. The manuscript now describes both conventions. Equation (57) and Lemma 11(ii) also admit different readings of which rank-$\alpha$ subsets must be active. I checked the printed Example 2 directly. At order two only the pair $\{2,5\}$ is active, and all third differences vanish. In the new counterexample every pair is active for every nonzero scale on its two axes. Both readings therefore fail. [Kászonyi, p. 317](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1506/download/10_5486_PMD_2010_4680.pdf#page=5)

The claimed exact equality has mathematical content even if the source’s historical goal was to construct grammars for subclasses. For $n=15$, the rank-one array $e(i)=\mathbf1_{3\mid i}+\mathbf1_{5\mid i}$ satisfies the noncrossing comparisons at $(0,5)$ and $(7,10)$ in the form of the source’s (79). Increasing $e(1)$ by one preserves the four values $2,1,0,1$ and changes the mixed difference at 1 from 0 to 1. The same certificate thus admits two distinct ranks. This identifies the missing containment in the published argument independently of its typography. [Kászonyi, p. 327](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1506/download/10_5486_PMD_2010_4680.pdf#page=15)

## Attempts to falsify the mathematics

- **The binary witness.** I computed the alternating sums in cyclic word order, without transforming to grid coordinates. For the seed, all 44 admissible pair-scale combinations gave the stated values at zero, and all 5,040 admissible triple boxes over the 105 origins vanished. The seed has gap sum 23 and zeroth gap 5. For every natural $t$, the two letter counts are coprime because $23(105t+1)\equiv23\pmod{105}$. I also generated the five literal words for $0\le t\le4$ and checked proper block lengths directly. The infinitely many members, together with the absence of nonzero sparse periods, supply the actual contradiction.
- **Arbitrary prime multiplicities.** I derived the sparse-period classification directly on the cyclic indices. At a primitive $n$th root of unity the full difference has nonzero multiplier at frequency 1. Hence the Fourier sum of any allowable period vanishes there. A nonzero real vector on at most two indices can cancel only at equal-weight antipodes. For even $n$ and $\alpha\le k-2$, a difference on $\alpha+1$ odd axes also has nonzero multiplier at frequency 2, where the antipodal sum is nonzero. For even $n$ at rank $k-1$, the order-two axis kills each antipodal pair. The revised manuscript includes this calculation. I also checked the distinct-side grid induction and its equality case. Independent enumeration of the unit-difference matrix columns gave exactly the predicted sparse directions for all proper ranks at $n=6,12,15,18,30,45,75,90,105,210,420$.
- **All context-free sublanguages.** Finite-state tagging recovers the ordered gap exponents even when neighboring marked letters have zero gap. Each period in a contained linear component lies in the mixed-difference kernel by subtraction. In the exceptional case every two distinct antipodal pairs cross, leaving each component with a single direction. A finitely generated additive submonoid on that ray is a finite union of progressions. The seed plus sufficiently many copies of any one antipodal direction supplies an infinite progression inside the exact primitive class. Outside the exceptional case every such component is a singleton. This proves both directions, including the existence assertion.
- **Formal targets.** I built the five binary-word modules from source with Lean 4.36.0 in a fresh temporary directory, using the existing Mathlib dependencies. The build included `page328_intersection`, with an actual word decoder, the exponent set of the primitive slice, and their exact set intersection. It also included `existential_rank_refutation`. The two rank readings have distinct formal targets. I inspected the separate Rust verification record for those targets and the grid lemma. It reports 6,792 checked declarations and the three standard axioms `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. The language-theoretic classification for arbitrary $n$ is proved in the manuscript.

## Scholarly value and priority

Ginsburg and Spanier’s Theorem 2.3 already characterizes the existence of an infinite bounded context-free sublanguage by sparse periods. Their Theorem 2.1 supplies the stratified representation, and their four-block example illustrates crossing periods. Downset-code and footprint results supply the grid distance and its equality case. The contribution here is the determination of the periods and sublanguages of this particular family, which reverses a published prediction across its entire range. The revised manuscript credits these ingredients. [Ginsburg and Spanier, pp. 291–295](https://msp.org/pjm/1966/16-2/pjm-v16-n2-p09-p.pdf#page=8) · [Srinivasan, Tripathi and Venkitesh, Lemma 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1908.07215) · [Geil, Theorem 7 and Corollary 9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.06613)

I found no earlier equivalent refutation in the sources checked. Cadilhac’s October 2026 preprint reduces rational polynomial solvability to the general decision problem for stratifiability. The November 2026 paper by Howe and coauthors constructs circuits for primitive words of fixed total length and for unions of exact power orders. Its use of prime-coordinate grids concerns a different invariant. The 1999 PUMA paper on decomposing the primitive slice and the 2014 Dömösi–Ito chapter remain the principal coverage limitations. I did not obtain their full texts. First priority therefore remains provisional.
