# Question, antecedents and literature coverage

The paper immediately preceding Lemma 11 on printed page 328 asks for stratified semilinearity of its exact intermediate-rank primitive classes. The 2010 introduction places the work in a research programme to construct grammars for subclasses of primitive words. Section 5 and Example 2 confirm that absence of higher interactions is part of the class. The construction in the present study meets both readings of the lower-order nonvanishing condition. Two notational inconsistencies in Definitions 4 and 6 are resolved explicitly using the adjacent lemmas in the primary source. [Kászonyi, 2010](https://doi.org/10.5486/PMD.2010.4680) · [Original example, p. 329](https://publi.math.unideb.hu/paper/1506/download/10_5486_PMD_2010_4680.pdf#page=17)

The distinction between an exact rank and an ambient cover matters even at 105 marked letters. Kászonyi and Katsura had already given a grammar result for the entire slice at this parameter. The lower-rank proof in the 2010 paper places vectors in larger sets defined by inequalities which witness primitivity. Our proof shows why those sets cannot represent the exact lower-rank classes. Sparse periods, earlier coding-theory distances, and the criterion for a semilinear bounded language to contain an infinite context-free subset are established results. The contribution is the negative answer for this published family, the all-parameter primitive witnesses, the correction to the rank-one assertion and the description of all the exceptional sublanguages. [Kászonyi and Katsura](https://doi.org/10.5486/PMD.1997.1707) · [Ginsburg and Spanier, Theorems 2.1 and 2.3](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.1966.16.285) · [Srinivasan, Tripathi and Venkitesh](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.07215) · [Geil](https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.06613)

On 27 November 2026 we checked the original PDF, the journal's issue titles from 2010 through 2026, indexed citation and update records, research by the original authors, and later work on related languages. Exact-title, DOI, author, strata, semiperiodicity and erratum searches found no earlier equivalent refutation. The accessible records of the 1999 PUMA article describe a proposed decomposition. The 2014 monograph includes a detailed chapter on the Kászonyi–Katsura theory. We checked that chapter's indexed excerpts and the earlier article's abstract. Their complete texts were unavailable in this search. These limits preclude an exhaustive claim of priority. Recent primary sources address related aspects of primitive words and stratification. Amarilli and coauthors study probability evaluation. Cadilhac studies the general stratifiability decision problem. Howe and coauthors study Boolean circuits for primitive words with fixed word length. None of these papers gives the exact prime-difference classification examined here. The £200 offer in Shallit's slides concerns the full binary primitive-word language. We found no formal reward applicable to the intermediate classes.
