# Independent mathematical review of the proposed answer to Canonne Q1

Reviewed 27 November 2026. This is an internal adversarial review of the mathematics and specification. I inspected the Lean statements, definitions and selected bridges, the main manuscript, the five companion sections, and the independent arithmetic checks described below. I did not certify the kernel replay or independently reproduce every tactic proof.

## Verdict

I found no counterexample to the finite same-radius characterization, its closed arithmetic proxy, or the countable finite-projection characterization. The finite functional meets the explicit-input computational interpretation of succinctness. The countable projection formula is well-defined and test-free. The direct countable formula requires an additional mathematical argument, provided below. The original draft's final remarks on infinite tail expressions do not by themselves prove this stronger assertion. The executable also has an input/output limitation documented below.

The original [COLT Open Question 1, p. 4](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf#page=4) permits universal constant-factor characterization and identifies closed form or efficient evaluation on explicitly given laws as alternatives. The paper's testing definition on p. 1 uses ordered IID observations, randomized tests, a strict TV alternative, and two separate one-third error constraints. Its discussion on p. 2 explicitly includes countably infinite domains.

## Statistical statement and proof

- In the finite Lean model, `CanonneNearOneTesting.GoodTest` is a bounded rejection function on all ordered tuples with null rejection at most one third and alternative rejection at least two thirds for every normalized nonnegative PMF at strict half-L1 distance greater than the specified radius. The countable model in `CountableProduct.lean` uses the same event and the product PMF. `CanonneCountable.finite_good_test_iff` proves agreement on finite alphabets. Both allow budget zero. At equality in the empty-alternative check, constant acceptance is correct. Declared zero labels can change that branch and are retained.
- `finiteRate` uses no test, alternative PMF, or risk minimization. Its choices are a sorted split, a scalar root and a least dyadic exponent. `CanonicalSplit` requires the prefix before its last atom to be strictly insufficient. Ties may change label identities but cannot change the scalar masses. The classical sorted choice is independent of any test. `countableRate` is the least integer upper bound on the explicitly defined finite rates, with boundedness proved. The separate quantities `sampleComplexity` and `countableSampleComplexity` contain tests, precisely on the comparator side.
- The pilot lower bound uses a single channel on the *whole record*. Repeated slots share the same pilot labels. Conditional on the pilot lists it produces IID inputs under each reduced component. The null approximation is charged once, and data processing compares two outputs of the same random channel. The deterministic support-overlap bound survives arbitrary changes to the marker probability. The signed shifts conserve total mass, can operate with a zero marker, and have head loss at least one thirty-second of the off-largest profile. The marker tilt and the signed prior are separate necessary conditions on every successful budget. Their conjunction does not require simultaneous occurrence of two different priors.
- The upper proof charges three batches for the capped overlap test and at most four additional head/mass batches on the regular branch. Its overlap condition uses the full law with arbitrary alternative tail mass. The direction of the head-deficit inequality is consistent with strict TV. The lower baseline interpolation requires a genuinely far witness, which is supplied before using it.
- In the horizontal argument, the baseline amplification is exactly $2^{43}$. If $nY<1$, the marker inequality gives $nwA\le32776^2$; comparing $w$ with $1/2$ then contradicts one of $ne,n\delta\ge2^{32}$. In the other case the two coefficient bounds and the floor give $1+40(4097)=163881$. The dilation uses only $K,c$, with the smallest head mass bounding every tail atom and the head overshoot. In the light-tail case $X(Mn)=M^2X(n)$ because every summand stays unsaturated at both clipped roots. Multiplication of the sample budget leaves the distance unchanged. The displayed upper factor 49152 applies to the unrounded finite profile and the countable supremum; the rounded profile has its own absolute factors.
- At a *fixed* budget, the reverse projection implication selects one pointwise-convergent subsequence in the compact countable product cube. This choice is made before fixing the alternative. Each fixed strictly far law has strictly far projections for all sufficiently large indices, so its inequality passes along that same subsequence by bounded convergence. Pointwise convergence at each law suffices for the universal assertion. Conditional lifting handles cells with zero null mass. The auxiliary half-radius argument only establishes existence of a finite budget. The final comparison uses the original radius throughout. The countable proof treats randomized rejection functions in the usual sample-complexity model; it gives no uniform runtime or implementation for arbitrary representations of infinite laws.

## Direct countable expression and endpoint checks

Here is a proof that the direct, unrounded atom formula is also a characterization, if one wishes to make that claim. On the declared alphabet of natural numbers, radius one is vacuous. For $0<e<1$, there is a label with mass below $\delta=1-e$, and eventually its singleton is exposed. Order **positive** atoms by descending mass, allowing ties. If infinitely many atoms are positive and even one atom has zero mass, there is no decreasing enumeration of *all* labels. The zero labels have their usual role in the experiment and contribute nothing to the sums.

Let $R_m$ denote the finite unrounded profile of the first $m$ labels and one remainder cell. Write $t_m$ for its mass. Let $R_*$ denote the main unrounded profile formed directly from the full mass sequence. I claim $R_m=R_*$ for all sufficiently large $m$. With finite positive support, eventually all positive atoms are exposed and the pooled zero cell supplies any needed zero label. Additional zero singletons do not affect the profile.

For infinite positive support and $0<e<1$, the positive sorted head is finite because its target $\delta<1$. Its last mass $a$ is positive. Eventually every label of mass at least $a$ has been exposed and $t_m<a$. The head mass multiset, its overshoot, both group masses and every head cost then agree with the direct formula. This includes the case where the head ends within a run of ties.

If $f(0)=0$ and $f(v)/v$ is nondecreasing on positive $v$, pooling increases its sum since $f(u)+f(v)\le f(u+v)$. Apply this to $v\min(1,\sqrt{v/\delta})$. The excess caused by the pooled cell is between zero and $t_m$, so $J_m\downarrow J$. If $J=16\delta$, equality belongs to the near branch and all such projections also use that branch. If $J<16\delta$, the projections eventually choose the regular branch. On the near branch $1/J_m\uparrow1/J$, so the integer ceilings stabilize even when $1/J$ is an integer.

Once the head has stabilized, apply pooling to $f(v)=\min(v/x,nv^{3/2}/\sqrt x)$ at fixed $n,x>0$. The excess is at most $t_m/x$, giving $K_m(n,x)\downarrow K(n,x)$. For a positive tail, the latter function of $x$ is continuous, strictly decreasing, and has limits infinity and zero: a single positive atom gives the first limit, the total mass divided by $x$ gives the second, and domination on compact intervals gives continuity. Monotonicity gives $X_m(n)\ge X(n)$. For each $h>0$, evaluating the convergence of $K_m$ at $X(n)+h$ puts the projected root below $X(n)+h$ eventually. Thus $X_m(n)\downarrow X(n)$.

At the direct first dyadic crossing, the finite inequality holds once the head is fixed because the projected right-hand side is larger. At each preceding power it eventually fails by strict failure at the limit. Only finitely many preceding powers occur. This proves the stabilization claim, including equality at the crossing. The direct crossing exists by taking the full finite head and using positive initial slack. The tail radical **minimum** at a fixed sample size is attained on the finite saturated prefix (or the empty prefix). A **supremum** over tail prefixes occurs instead after rewriting the budget threshold as intersecting constraints in the closed-exponent formula.

The projected statistical complexities are nondecreasing under refinement by conditional lifting, are bounded integers, and have supremum equal to the countable optimum by the fixed-budget equivalence. Thus they eventually equal that optimum. Passing the finite pair of inequalities along the eventually constant sequences proves both inequalities for $R_*$ with the original constants. This does **not** identify the early-prefix supremum of the *profiles* with their eventual value. It proves statistical comparability of both. This direct characterization is a paper-level corollary unless separately formalized.

## Closed formula, bit complexity and executable

The rounded head maximum is within a factor eight of the sum of the four original terms with individual losses $1,2,2^{7/4},1$. The unrounded objective is concave along a tied head run, so endpoint compression suffices for this estimate even if the rounded minimizer differs. For the tail, the saturated prefix realizes equality in the quadratic root bound, giving the factor 16 after the square-root rounding. Taking the maximum with the overshoot gives the factor 17. The rounded near threshold deliberately overlaps the regular lower proof's range $J<32\delta$. The branch may differ from the unrounded statistical profile. A comparison of the two profiles as statistically equivalent, not as identical integers, is appropriate.

For $n=4^k$, distributing the finite minimum/maximum converts each constraint into a union of two upward integer intervals. Intersecting these unions over tail prefixes and numerator terms yields the declared max-min exponent, including zero denominators and degree zero. Fixed rational powers under dyadic rounding require only shifts and bounded-degree integer powers. Numerator and denominator bit lengths, the exponent *value* $k$, and hence the binary output length are polynomial in the input length. The time bound is for evaluating the rate and does not bound the testing algorithm's runtime.

I independently compared `closed_rate.rate` with a direct power-of-four scan of the rounded inequalities. The reference check evaluated the powers by repeated doubling/halving, expanded tied atoms, and explicitly retained the permitted endpoints. All **5,538** comparisons agreed, including 1,908 empty, 3,027 regular, and 603 near-maximum evaluations. These examples check implementation consistency and do not certify statistical optimality.

I found one reproducible CLI defect in the initial version. On CPython with its default 4,300-digit conversion cap, take two atoms of mass $1/2$ and $e=2^{-8000}$. The input denominator has only 2,409 decimal digits. The function returns exponent 7999 and a profile with 15,999 binary digits, but `json.dumps` raises `ValueError` on that valid output. A local trusted-input CLI can disable the conversion cap before parsing; alternatively support unbounded binary/hex input and output. This is a deliverable issue, not a counterexample to the complexity argument.

The manuscript inputs originally cited `paninski2008`, `valiantvaliant2017`, and `chhorcarpentier2022`, while the main bibliography used different keys and lacked Paninski. This review neither establishes priority nor records acceptance by the problem's author.

## Post-correction verification

I reran the actual CLI as separate CPython processes, each starting with the 4,300-digit conversion cap explicitly enabled. With two atoms of mass one half, input radii 2^-8000 and 2^-16000 produced valid JSON. I compared the decoded results to the exact independently computed integers. The returned exponents were 7,999 and 15,999, with outputs of 15,999 and 31,999 bits respectively. The second test also requires reading a denominator with 4,817 decimal digits. Both input and output regression cases passed.

I checked all six citation keys in the assembled TeX against its bibliography. All resolve, and the final TeX log has no undefined-reference warning. I read the rendered 16-page PDF with PDFKit. It includes the direct countable argument, Paninski reference, and corrected PMLR pagination 5312–5316, with no unresolved double-question-mark placeholders. The page range also agrees with the locally recorded official proceedings metadata.

The revised countable section gives the stabilization argument, including positive-atom ordering, finite support on an infinite declared alphabet, the closed near branch at equality, the one-sided convergence of projected roots, and strict failure at every earlier dyadic budget. It uses an infimum for the direct tail expression and deduces the comparison constants by eventual equality of the statistical budgets. The verification section explicitly identifies countable stabilization and the polynomial bit bound as paper proofs. The outstanding objections from the original review are resolved. I found no remaining serious mathematical objection within the scope reviewed.
