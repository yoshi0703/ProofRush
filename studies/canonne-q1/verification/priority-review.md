# Independent scope and priority review

Public sources checked on 27 November 2026 (JST). I reviewed the statement of the proposed theorem and its functional independently of the proof team. This review assesses correspondence with the original problem, identifiable prior research, and the published prize terms. The mathematical proof and the Lean verification have their own reviews.

## Does the theorem answer Open Question 1?

Yes, if the stated two-sided theorem and rational evaluation result hold. Canonne asks for the smallest number of i.i.d. draws from an arbitrary unknown law $p$ that suffice to distinguish $p=q$ from $d_{\rm TV}(p,q)>\varepsilon$, with each error at most $1/3$. The proposed theorem bounds the number above and below by absolute constant multiples of an explicit functional of $q,\varepsilon$, using the same $\varepsilon\in(0,1]$ in the alternative and in both comparisons. It includes zero-probability labels and the zero-sample case when the strict alternative is empty. These choices match §1 and Open Question 1 in §5. The original §1 says it treats finite domains in its definition of total variation, and §2 uses sequences on $\mathbb N$ to express its functionals. The extension to a countable alphabet removes any ambiguity over the scope of that notation. [Canonne, COLT 2024, §§1–2 and 5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf)

The proposal splits the distribution at the exact tail-mass quantile. Its sample bound combines a truncated head moment with a crossing involving a tail-overlap quantity, and uses a clipped $3/2$-moment near distance one. This is a direct function of the known null and the distance. The finite rational result gives a version computable in polynomial time in the input bit length, which meets the author's stated preference for efficient evaluation from an explicit description. The countable characterization uses a finite-projection supremum and establishes a finite mathematical value. Its evaluation depends on how the infinite law is represented. The size of the absolute comparison constants affects numerical precision in applications and leaves the requested constant-factor statement intact. [Original discussion of succinctness and constant factors](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf#page=4)

The result would settle the sample-complexity formulation for arbitrary reference laws. The substantive advance is eliminating the unbounded sample-size gap caused by moving the distance threshold, including at sharp changes in the number of observations needed. Existing instance-sensitive weighted tests and tail perturbations belong in the attribution. The new claim concerns the universal fixed-distance combination of the terms and the proof of both comparisons. [Canonne, §§2–3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf)

## Closest public research

- **Valiant and Valiant (FOCS 2014, journal 2017).** Their opening claim of instance-optimality explicitly allows a constant change in separation as well as in sample count. In Canonne's normalization the lower and upper bounds in equation (1) truncate at $\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon/16$. On a harmonic law, the index retaining a prescribed mass is exponential in that mass times the harmonic sum, so this shift can cause a polynomial gap in the sample count. Their truncated $2/3$-moment and weighted testing constructions are substantive prior art. [Valiant and Valiant's paper](https://theory.stanford.edu/~valiant/papers/instanceOpt.pdf) · [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1137/151002526) · [Canonne, §2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf#page=2)
- **Blais, Canonne and Gur (CCC 2017).** Theorems 30 and 33 use a Peetre $K$-functional and yield upper and lower estimates at different truncation scales. Their comparison includes the harmonic example. The inverse Peetre functional in that work and the proposed tail-overlap function take different inputs and compare different quantities. I found no result there establishing a fixed-distance sample-count characterization. [Blais, Canonne and Gur, Theorems 30 and 33](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/entities/document/10.4230/LIPIcs.CCC.2017.28) · [Canonne, §2](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf#page=3)
- **Local minimax separation.** Balakrishnan and Wasserman, Chhor and Carpentier, and Lam-Weil, Carpentier and Sriperumbudur obtain local minimax results in related multinomial settings. The closest result of Chhor and Carpentier bounds the separation radius at a given sample count up to constants. Inverting that comparison still permits a shift in separation. Canonne expressly identifies this discrepancy in §3. [Balakrishnan and Wasserman](https://arxiv.org/abs/1706.10003) · [Chhor and Carpentier](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/28933?nt=1) · [Lam-Weil, Carpentier and Sriperumbudur](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.01219) · [Canonne, §3](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf#page=3)
- **Dewasurendra, *Closing the Harmonic Gap* (2026).** This author-posted preprint already claims a matching characterization at the same $\varepsilon$ for the harmonic example and its Zipfian generalization. Its Theorem 1 requires a fixed $\kappa$-Zipf envelope $1/(\kappa L i)\le q_i\le\kappa/(L i)$, $0<\varepsilon\le1/3$, and a lower bound on the effective quantile $m_q(\varepsilon)$. The constants may depend on $\kappa$. The argument uses the exact tail quantile, a coarsened head test, a tail collision statistic, and a graded sparse-thinning prior. Section 6 explicitly proposes combining the head's $2/3$-moment with retention of part of the tail for a future general solution. The proposed universal formula and common-channel construction go beyond the theorem in that preprint. Any account of the contribution should credit this overlap in approach and the previously claimed harmonic rate $\Theta_\varepsilon((\log k)k^{(1-\varepsilon)/2})$. The first public GitHub commit and the Zenodo entry record 12 August 2026. The current PDF identifies the author as Pahan Dewasurendra. [Author's PDF, Theorem 1 and §§4–7](https://andotheror.github.io/pdf/closing-the-harmonic-gap-exact-identity-testing-for-zipfian-distributi.pdf) · [First source commit](https://github.com/andotheror/harmonic-gap-identity-testing-zipfian/commit/a004fe10be) · [Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/22065366)
- **Nearby problem statements.** Canonne's later work with Blanc and Waingarten considers detecting departures from a uniform law and competing with knowledge of the alternative profile in hindsight. The July 2026 note posted by Edward Yu proposes a repair for Open Question 3, the corner case of the Valiant–Valiant lower bound. The latter does not provide a matching upper bound at the same distance. Neither result supplies the two-sided functional required for Question 1. [Blanc, Canonne and Waingarten](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.02637) · [Yu's posted Q3 proposal](https://quant.am/statistics/2026/07/22/closing-open-question-3/)

## Public priority evidence and its limits

I found no publicly inspectable proof or equivalent theorem covering arbitrary $q$ at the specified distance with uniform constants. The public search covered the problem title, citation key and synonyms such as “massively parameterized identity testing,” “fixed-radius sample complexity,” and “local minimax multinomial goodness of fit.” I also searched for the distinctive tail quantile, sample-support overlap, clipped moment, collision and water-filling terms in combinations relevant to the proposed formula. I followed the research papers and author repositories surfaced by these searches and checked Canonne's institutional and personal publication lists, ECCC, and public results from automated research. The OpenTCS entry is labeled Unverified. That is the catalog's status and carries no ruling from Canonne. [Author publications](https://ccanonne.github.io/publications.html) · [University of Sydney research outputs](https://profiles.sydney.edu.au/clement.canonne/publications) · [ECCC bibliography](https://eccc.weizmann.ac.il/author/602/) · [OpenTCS problem entry](https://www.opentcs.cc/problems/tight-characterization-of-instance-optimal-identity-testing/)

There is one material unresolved lead. The Institute of Foundation Models explicitly lists this exact COLT problem as the subject of one Rethlas generator record and 16 verifier sessions. Its public card gives aggregate generator statuses across all problems, and the verifier entries count sessions. Neither gives the outcome of the Canonne run or the scope of any resulting claim. The underlying files require the requester to share their name, affiliation and email with the dataset authors. I did not request access. The public FrenzyMath results repository and Rethlas/Danus papers did not disclose a matching proof. The linked SolveAll problem page was unavailable in this search, and the site root redirected to a sign-in service. The lead leaves earlier unpublished or gated work possible. I also checked the October 2026 OpenAI mathematics release. A search of the catalogue, overview source and complete preprint-directory listing at commit `3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6` found the homonymous noncommutative polynomial identity-testing result and no entry for statistical identity testing of discrete distributions. This is a catalogue and title check. Work under unrelated terminology or outside the public indexes can escape it. [Release announcement](https://openai.com/index/sharing-ai-progress-in-mathematics/) · [Pinned OpenAI catalogue](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/3344da59991ed94f9ba711217b29c1d760badfa6/CONTENTS.md)

The evidence supports describing the work as a proposed resolution of Open Question 1 and reporting that this review found no earlier public general solution. Saying that the problem was definitely unsolved, that this is the first solution, or that the author has accepted the proof would exceed this evidence.

## Prize and AI involvement

Section 5 of Canonne's 2024 note offers AUD 200 for resolving Open Question 1. The wombat and food prizes are for Questions 2 and 3. The notice gives no expiry, submission procedure, peer-review or Lean requirement, AI eligibility rule, or judging panel. I found no public update confirming that the offer remains available or stating how an AI-assisted solution would be assessed. I also checked the accessible portion of Canonne's public Bluesky feed covering November 2025 through November 2026. He is the natural contact to determine how the offer is assessed. This is an inference from the publication. This literature audit involved no external contact.
