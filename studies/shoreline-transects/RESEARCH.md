# Research record for endpoint inclusion and repeated transects in shoreline litter monitoring

**26 November 2026 UTC.** The English [working paper](paper.pdf) and its [source](paper.tex) document an exact sampling result and two observational cohorts. The research question was whether surveying and potentially clearing four strips changes the visible debris on subsequent visits. The data also allow a direct check of how often crews recorded the end strips. The field mechanism, effect of cleanup and priority of the statistical result remain open. The work has not undergone external peer review or been submitted to a meeting.

## What the numbers measure

Candidate selectors draw four distinct starts from 0, 5, …, 95 metres on a nominal 100 m site. Under the 2021 spreadsheet formula, the two end positions each have three raw integer preimages, while all 18 interior positions have five. The guide advises retrying duplicates. It does not choose explicitly between repeating the whole batch and retaining previous unique draws. The educator guide separately gives a uniform raw generator. For that alternative the study completes the draw to four distinct positions. The supplied NOAA lists are another option whose generator is unknown. Each class of implementation is an assumption concerning an individual field survey. [2021 spreadsheet instruction, printed p. 8](https://repository.library.noaa.gov/view/noaa/33282/noaa_33282_DS1.pdf#page=8) · [Educator guidance](https://marinedebris.noaa.gov/before-every-survey-safety-tips-and-transect-selection)

Eligible records have one survey ID per site and date, protocol 2.0, exactly four distinct permitted starts, 5 m widths and complete nonnegative counts on the main beach. Zeros count. Back-barrier observations are separate. The extracted data do not say which random generator was used. The endpoint frequency per visit is the number of sampled end positions divided by two. The primary site-weighted frequency first averages all eligible visits for each site and then averages sites equally. A recorded count combines local abundance and detectability. [NOAA data use and documentation](https://marinedebris.noaa.gov/our-work/monitoring/marine-debris-monitoring-and-assessment-project) · [Within-strip detection study](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.marpolbul.2023.115905)

The two bootstrap intervals describe site variation in these cohorts. Volunteer selection, record availability, site access and dependence across nearby sites are further sources of uncertainty. The pilot interval includes the 20% uniform reference, and the held-out interval lies below it. The difference between ordinary and reweighted mean debris counts is at most 0.222 and 0.069 items per strip, respectively. The sign and size of actual field bias depend on the unrecorded selector and on debris outside observed strips. The simple extreme example in the paper concerns a hypothetical population concentrated at the two ends.

The 263 eligible pilot visits recorded 20,210 items, between 6 May 2021 and 30 August 2026. Of 503 pairs adjacent in the raw site chronologies, 236 had two eligible visits and 151 were also 21–42 days apart. Eleven of these 151 pairs repeated exactly the same four starts. Every one occurred at public site ID 1283. The frequency supplies evidence of an unresolved site-specific schedule or recording pattern. The expected number of such pairs under any of the three independent candidate draws is below 0.04, conditional on visit eligibility not selecting for repeated sets. Reasons for the repetitions are unknown. The pilot previous-visit contrast was −1.60 items per strip across 17 sites (descriptive 95% site interval −4.33 to 1.02). Substituting the next visit's labels gave +1.79 (−0.85 to 5.11). An item can be removed outside surveyed strips. The records omit some intervening cleanups, weather, access and observer effort. An actual removal effect or deposition rate remains unidentified.

## Proof, reproducibility and priority

* The sampling result assumes a common fixed number of distinct labels, a nonempty endpoint stratum, at least one selected interior label, and exchangeability within each stratum for each law. The observer sees only unordered labels and their counts, with no generator identifier or equipment record. Counts are held fixed for the single visit. The general necessity and sufficiency proof and its extension to randomized rules by conditioning appear in the [paper](paper.pdf).
* For arbitrary sampling families on finite sample and label sets, a linear target is simultaneously estimable without design bias precisely when each label has a system of feasible sample-dependent weights. Necessity follows by subtracting the all-zero response from the response to one unit impulse at that label. Sufficiency follows by interchanging the sums for the weighted estimator. This general identity allows variable sample size and requires no within-stratum symmetry. Godambe introduced sample-dependent weights in finite-population estimation. Our small two-stratum matrix is a separate simplification under the fixed-size exchangeability assumptions above. [General Lean identity](lean/LinearEstimator.lean) · [Godambe, 1955](https://academic.oup.com/jrsssb/article/17/2/269/7026727)
* The conditional-product and successive designs have a substantial prior literature. Chen, Dempster and Liu give the product-of-weights model. Yu gives both draw rules and orders their inclusion probabilities. Horvitz and Thompson prove unbiasedness when inclusion probabilities are known. We apply these methods to the published shoreline formula and derive the unknown-selector criterion and worked bounds. The literature search did not establish priority for the criterion as a general theorem of survey sampling. [Chen et al., 1994](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/81.3.457) · [Yu, 2012](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4107) · [Horvitz and Thompson, 1952](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1952.10483446)
* The concrete 20-strip impossibility holds for every real-valued response rule on a single observed unordered sample and nonnegative integer counts. The sharp endpoint-component minimax errors for signed, nonnegative and nondecreasing nonnegative coefficients are 0.5310%, 18.2054% and 19.9739%. The paper extends the 18.2054% lower bound to arbitrary finite nonnegative estimators through populations concentrated at the endpoints. The general criterion and this last limiting argument are mathematical proofs in the paper. [Lean model](lean/Shoreline.lean) · [Formal component-bias bounds](lean/EndpointBias.lean)
* A second implementation enumerated every one of the 4,845 possible four-sets and their 24 orderings. That arithmetic connects the two literal candidate draw procedures to the probability laws used in Lean. Rust `nanoda` checked the exported dependency closure for eight Lean targets, comprising 9,157 declarations, under macOS Seatbelt. An independent second export confirmed the general infinite-family, finite-sample calibration identity across 7,300 declarations. The two dependency closures overlap. The reported assumptions were Lean's standard `propext`, `Classical.choice` and `Quot.sound`. These checks establish properties of the specified games of chance. They do not identify a generator from NOAA records. The most discriminating next test is a prospectively logged series from an independent site or archive. For each visit it would record the generator, intended and realized positions, duplicate handling, selected-strip and whole-site cleanup, and intervening cleanups. Blinded duplicate counts would estimate measurement error. These records would distinguish selective sampling from data-entry conventions and allow a defensible estimate of a previous-visit association under a specified design. A causal comparison of cleanup strategies would also require an identified treatment assignment. The current study contributes a testable protocol audit, a mathematical limitation, and a replication of the descriptive field diagnostic. It does not resolve a previously named mathematical conjecture or the wider environmental problem.
