# Q2 / MathOverflow 332062: focused priority audit

Checked 27 November 2026. I located no earlier published or publicly posted general direct proof of the advertised two-sided Φ/Ψ comparison, nor of its proposed sharp constant. This is a scoped negative search result, **not confirmation of novelty, acceptance, correctness, or present award eligibility**. I have not independently checked the full proof or its claimed sharpness. The question's author ultimately judges whether a proof answers Q2.

## Original problems and exact consequences

The author asked the concrete comparison on [MathOverflow 332062, 20 May 2019](https://mathoverflow.net/questions/332062/relation-between-two-different-functionals-lvert-p-max-varepsilon-rve), for nonincreasing probability sequences p∈ℓ1. Φ removes the largest atom and a tail of mass ≤ε, then takes the ℓ_(2/3) quasinorm; Ψ is the inverse K-functional level κ_p^−1(1−ε). Equation (4) states, for ε∈(0,1/9), the **indirect** comparison

    1 ∨ Φ(9ε,p) ≲ 1 ∨ Ψ(ε,p) ≲ 1 ∨ [Φ(ε/32,p)/ε].

That thread currently shows exactly one answer, by Iosif Pinelis on 21 May 2019. It gives the counterexample p=(1,0,...) to the uncorrected assertion Ψ≤CΦ; the author clarifies the need for nondegeneracy or an additive/maximum baseline. It is not a direct proof of both inequalities in (4). Read the full answer and comments rather than interpreting an answer count as resolution.

The [COLT 2024 statement, §5](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v247/canonne24a/canonne24a.pdf) asks for a direct, non-contrived relationship between the two functionals. Q2's offered object is an extra-large wombat plush toy; Q1's AUD 200 concerns a different, more demanding sample-complexity characterization. Q2 is open-ended rather than a fully formal exact constant theorem; it is therefore prudent to state the mathematical inequality and prove its implication for the concrete MO equation rather than assert author acceptance or eligibility.

Assuming the proposed bounds Φ(ε)≤Ψ(ε)≤sqrt(1+[Φ(η)/(4(ε−η))]^2) for 0<η<ε<1, monotonicity gives the left MO bound: Φ(9ε)≤Φ(ε)≤Ψ(ε). On the right, η=ε/32 gives Ψ(ε)≤1+(8/31)Φ(ε/32)/ε. Thus, if proved with definitions matching the source, the proposed result implies the precise displayed MO (4) directly, with explicit constants, and does not require the author's small-ℓ2 assumption once 1∨ is included. The new inequality also covers levels outside ε<1/9. It does *not* imply a constant-factor same-radius upper Ψ(ε)≤C(1∨Φ(ε)); the gap η<ε matters, including at tail cutoffs. Numerical statements about sharpness must be independently checked against the actual truncation convention.

## Most relevant original interpolation sources

* [Montgomery-Smith, *The distribution of Rademacher sums* (1990)](https://stephenmontgomerysmith.github.io/preprints/tail.pdf), Lemma 1, already gives the exact duality κ_p(t)=sup{Σ p_i z_i: ||z||∞≤1, ||z||₂≤t}; this is standard prior art and must not be presented as new. Its Lemma 2 compares K to a partition Q norm; the main theorem gives weighted Rademacher tail probabilities, not the present truncated ℓ_(2/3) comparison. The paper recalls Holmstedt's earlier decreasing-rearrangement approximation with constants.
* [Blais–Canonne–Gur, CCC 2017](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol079-ccc2017/LIPIcs.CCC.2017.28/LIPIcs.CCC.2017.28.pdf): Proposition 15 is Holmstedt's approximate expression for κ(t) in terms of the largest floor(t²) entries and the ℓ₂ tail, with a 1/4 factor on κ itself. That 1/4 is **not** the proposed sharp 1/4 in the new inverse-level estimate. Section 6.1 compares K and Q, §6.2 treats weighted Rademacher concentration, and §6.3 works out uniform and harmonic examples. §6.3 compares Ψ and Φ for those specific laws, not a general direct proof of equation (4). Truncation constants cannot simply be discarded when inverting approximate K bounds at a level close to 1.
* [Holmstedt, *Interpolation of quasi-normed spaces* (1970)](https://doi.org/10.7146/math.scand.a-10976), the original approximation underlying BCG Proposition 15, is substantive interpolation prior art; I did not obtain and inspect every original theorem or subsequent citation. Montgomery-Smith's paper reproduces the relevant K≈head-ℓ1+tail-ℓ2 estimate, not the claimed exact inverse/Φ inequality. General results in the interpolation literature could conceivably imply the comparison by a nontrivial specialization; this audit cannot rule that out.
* The available searches for Tikhomirov together with interpolation, truncated ℓ_(2/3), and this K-functional did not reveal an exact match. No claim is made that his approximation-theory work or its citation graph has been fully searched. The terms 'Tikhomirov' and 'Tikhonov regularization' are distinct, and no specific latter result is asserted here.
* [Edward Yu's 22 July 2026 post](https://quant.am/statistics/2026/07/22/closing-open-question-3/) proposes an argument for Canonne's separate Q3, the testing lower-bound corner case. It uses cutoff and block constructions, but the posted statement is not the two-sided analytic Q2 comparison and its proof needs independent review. The [Zipf manuscript](https://andotheror.github.io/pdf/closing-the-harmonic-gap-exact-identity-testing-for-zipfian-distributi.pdf) studies a structured testing subclass; it is not a general Q2 proof. Neither establishes that Q2 is unclaimed.

## Quantifiers and audit gates

For 0<ε<1 and summable p, the sorted tail eventually has mass ≤ε, so the retained Φ prefix is finite even with countably many nonzero atoms. The inverse level 1−ε lies strictly between 0 and 1. κ is continuous and nondecreasing, tends to 1 as t→∞, and is strictly increasing while its value is below 1, so the relevant inverse is unambiguous; at ε=0 the finite-support plateau changes the right-inverse convention, and at ε=1 the level is 0. The MO question states ε∈(0,1], whereas the proposed strict η<ε bound is naturally for 0<η<ε<1; endpoint ε=1 should be handled separately, not assumed covered. Zeros, ties, a point mass, and cutoff equality (tail mass exactly ε) deserve explicit tests. The natural threshold η=0 is a limiting assertion and is not available from a proof that assumes η>0 unless justified.

The first (lower) direction is a comparison at the same ε; the second is a mixed-radius bound and should not be paraphrased as same-radius equivalence. The statement Φ(ε)≤Ψ(ε) is stronger than MO's lower estimate, while the 1 inside the proposed upper square root directly accommodates Pinelis's point-mass example. The claimed universal optimality of 1/4 is a separate assertion: it needs an explicit admissible sequence with the correct order of limits and denominator, and is not established by BCG's unrelated constant.
