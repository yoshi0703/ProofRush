# Original statement and priority review

The open-problem session of the 37th Canadian Conference on Computational Geometry attributes Question 4 to Takumi Shiota of the University of Hyogo. The proceedings ask whether exactly a specified number of possibly overlapping induced side-two boundary cycles can have union equal to a given geometric grid graph's full edge set, and specifically ask for a polynomial algorithm or an NP-completeness result. Shiota's own slides give the same question and illustrate twelve placements. Neither source lists a cash award or an application process. [Conference record, pp. 373–374](https://cccg-wads-2025.eecs.yorku.ca/CCCG_2025_Open_Problems.pdf#page=3) · [Author's slides](https://shiotatakumi.github.io/MyPage/download/250814-CCCG-2025-Open-Problem.pptx)

**Input model.** In the bottom-left panel of Figure 4, count both coordinate lines from zero at the top left. The lattice vertices at (3,6) and (4,6) both occur on vertical edges and the horizontal unit edge between them is absent. The reduction likewise specifies its unit edges explicitly. Among a specified square's eight boundary vertices, the only possible ambient unit edges are its perimeter. For the figure an independent enumeration found 78 unit edges, 15 admissible squares and 11 forced placements. Two uncovered edges share a unique common placement. This yields exactly the 12 placements illustrated. [Figure 4](https://cccg-wads-2025.eecs.yorku.ca/CCCG_2025_Open_Problems.pdf#page=4) · [Formal induced-edge statement](../lean/GridInduced.lean)

**Source assumption.** The starting theorem explicitly supplies a plane, bendless orthogonal embedding on an integer grid for the NP-hard restricted Vertex Cover problem. Deleting isolated vertices preserves the optimum. Replacing used coordinate values by their ordered ranks preserves segment intersections, and reorienting each edge east or north gives the normalized input to the Lean theorem. No prescribed property of the target cover is imposed on the input drawing. The checks include both readings of repeated placements, binary budgets, the empty graph and the finite certificate size on arbitrary target instances. [Bergren et al., Theorem 4.3](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1336412) · [Source drawing definition](../lean/AxisGridSource.lean) · [Reduction theorem](../lean/ShiotaQuestionFour.lean)

**Existing results.** The Excel border-drawing problem cited by Shiota optimizes operations setting rectangular borders of different styles. The polygon-covering problem at FOCS 2024 covers a polygonal region with small pieces. The grid segment-covering theorem of Bergren and colleagues selects intervals incident to endpoints of segments. These objectives differ from selecting closed square perimeters with exactly the required edge union. [Iwama, Miyano and Ono](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00224-008-9117-y) · [Abrahamsen and Stade](https://ieee-focs.org/FOCS-2024-Papers/pdfs/FOCS2024-1oojWxXs5YAKfs3z3lBRMF/167400b355/167400b355.pdf) · [Bergren, Eiben, Ganian and Kanj](https://doi.org/10.1137/20M1336412)

**Search coverage, 27 November 2026.** A separate reviewer searched the question title, the author's publication list, CCCG 2026 accepted papers, English combinations of unit grid graphs, side-two square perimeters, induced eight-cycles and edge covers, and Japanese searches using the author's name and terms for grid, boundary and cover. This review found no equivalent published solution. Earlier results covering square interiors, disks or border styles do not supply the five local configurations or the exact edge cover equivalence. Public indexing is incomplete. The work has received internal computational and mathematical review, and external specialist review is pending.
