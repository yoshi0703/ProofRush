# Independent review of the two-parameter attention dimension result

## Lower bound

The coefficient count is correct for the enlarged common-denominator family. The denominator has H coefficients after setting its constant to one. There are d(H+1) numerator coefficients and B_d coefficients for the degree-at-most-D readout. At a fixed grid point, a term has D factors from the numerators or denominator and one readout coefficient, hence parameter degree at most D+1. Affine dependence in a normalized denominator can only reduce this degree. The strict-profile reduction changes the readout's constant, an allowed parameter that preserves the degree limit. [Hsu–Xu, §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04243)

The polynomial sign count must use varying input lengths and fixed H, D and d. For n at least HD, choosing HD of the n adjacent grid intervals gives exactly binomial(n,HD) distinct profiles when the first sign is fixed. Every chosen profile is implemented by the product of one simple-root factor per transition. Symmetrization preserves strict inequalities, gives a univariate polynomial in Hamming weight of no higher degree, and the intermediate value theorem forces one root in each transition interval. Thus those profiles have threshold degree exactly HD. The necessary inequality m ≥ HD follows by comparing growth exponents.

For H≥4 and d≤(D−2)/2, the argument gives B_d≥HD/2−d and B_(d+1)/B_d≥3. To make the last step explicit, HD/2 ≥ 2D ≥ 4d+4, so 3(HD/2−d)≥HD+1. The cases d>(D−2)/2 and H≤3 give the stated factor three. In the large-head result the central binomial bound follows by pairing the two central coefficients in row 2D−1. Its sum is 4^D/2 and the central coefficient in row 2D is their sum. The remaining strict step follows from D−2 < H/2.

The algebraic alternative needs an open neighborhood of missing monic coefficient vectors. A nonzero relation gives this neighborhood by continuity at a point where the relation is nonzero. Choose the target there with HD distinct roots inside (0,1), and then use continuity of the elementary symmetric functions to choose disjoint rational root windows such that every tuple of roots in those windows still has a coefficient vector in the neighborhood. Grid signs force any matching degree-at-most-HD polynomial to have one root per window. Monic normalization then puts its coefficients in the excluded neighborhood. This accounts for why one unrealizable polynomial by itself would be insufficient.

## Upper bounds

Ballico–Ellia Theorem 1 applies directly with genus 0, ambient dimension s≥3 and degree H≥s. It supplies a smooth rational curve of degree H and a surjective degree-D restriction map whenever B_s≥HD+1. Pullback along the parameterization identifies the target with all binary forms of degree HD. Since this is an existence argument it does not require proving that every generic parameterization is an embedding. The restriction matrix has entries polynomial with integer coefficients in the form coefficients. A nonzero maximal minor cannot vanish on all integer tuples. Dehomogenizing the resulting forms gives the shared features and the same homogeneous readout identity for every degree-at-most-HD polynomial. H≤2 is covered by the full exponent basis {0,…,H}. [Ballico–Ellia, Theorem 1](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1986__60_1_85_0.pdf) · [Larson, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.04906)

Hsu–Xu Lemma 11 allows any fixed distinct positive slopes. Its proof chooses the slopes before the target numerators. It therefore realizes the whole feature vector with one common positive denominator and unchanged H. The optional one-channel saving also follows. Require one of the s+1 forms, after dehomogenization, to have simple negative roots and positive constant. This is a nonempty real open condition. The nonzero full-rank minor remains nonzero at some point of this open set. Rescale that feature by a positive constant and use it as the common denominator. Dividing the homogeneous identity by its Dth power turns that coordinate into 1. The remaining s coordinates and the readout obtained by setting the first variable to 1 give the same signs with readout degree at most D. [Hsu–Xu, Lemmas 9–11](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04243)

The elementary mixed-radix route is consistent with Lemma 14. Put x=D/r, assume x≥32, and take p=4r. From H≤(e(1+x))^r and b−1<H^(1/p), we get 2p(b−1)≤8r[3(1+x)]^(1/4)≤D. The last inequality follows from x^4≥32^3x and 3·8^4(1+x)≤3·8^4(33/32)x. Thus the hypothesis 2p(b−1)≤D+2 is satisfied. The source needs at most D nonzero terms. Adding exponent zero pads the representations and makes the readout homogeneous. The resulting dimension bound is 2p+2≤10r, using r≥1. For x<32 the factor construction supplies d≤D<32r. [Hsu–Xu, Lemmas 12–14 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04243)

## Scope

The theorem concerns a worst case over all lengths and all symmetric targets of threshold degree at most HD in the fixed-query, position-free architecture. Section 6 asks how value dimension behaves in this setting. A uniform order characterization addresses that question at the level of asymptotic dependence. The argument leaves the exact integer threshold and function-specific questions such as parity open. The additive construction itself is from Hsu–Xu. The new part is the matching necessary dependence on H and D, together with the reduction from maximal rank for the sharper upper bound. [Hsu–Xu, §6](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04243)
