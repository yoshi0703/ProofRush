# Priority audit for the Hsu–Xu value-dimension question

Audit date 27 November 2026 JST (26 November UTC). This is the audit's independent argument; the final manuscript uses a sharper elementary constant and a separate checked Lean proof. In the targeted primary literature I found prior proofs of the available head–degree tradeoff, of the additive-basis upper construction, and of using algebraic sign-pattern counts for attention. I did not find a previously stated or proved worst-case dimension lower bound of the form below for the Hsu–Xu architecture. Priority remains conditional on the literature covered by this search.

## Exact question and scope

Daniel Hsu and Mingyue Xu consider a single fixed query, position-free softmax attention over binary inputs, a sum of \(H\) head outputs in the same \(d\)-dimensional value space, and a polynomial-threshold readout of degree at most \(D\). Their Theorem 2 gives \(HD\ge T\) for any target of threshold degree \(T\). Theorem 3 gives a universal symmetric-target dimension upper bound \(O(\min(D,\log H))\), with stronger estimates in some regimes. The paragraph after Theorem 3 and Section 6 ask whether that dimension can be improved at intermediate degree and whether it is necessary. Their separate min/max multitask problem costs value dimension times precision and uses linear readouts. [Hsu–Xu, arXiv:2608.04243, §§2–3, 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04243)

Let \(d_*(H,D)\) be the least dimension sufficient for **every** symmetric Boolean function of threshold degree at most \(HD\) at **every** input length. The representation parameters, including the readout, may vary with the function and length. Let \(r\) be the first positive integer with \(\binom{D+r}{r}\ge HD+1\). The proposed conclusion is \(d_* = \Theta(\min(D,r))\), with absolute constants, and in particular \(D-1\le d_*\le D\) for \(D\ge3\) and \(H\ge4^D\). The hard profiles have exactly \(HD\) changes and may require very large input lengths. These quantifiers do not give a lower bound for parity, for every symmetric target, or for a polynomial readout permitted to use position embeddings.

## Closest prior work

- **Rajaraman, Sundaram, and Tesfaye**, *The Head Complexity of Boolean Functions in Single-Layer Attention*, posted 3 September 2026. Their §5.2 applies Warren's strict sign-pattern theorem to one-layer attention after clearing denominators, and shifts the threshold to eliminate zero margins. These proof ingredients are directly prior. Their Theorem 7 uses \(2^n\) input polynomials and \(O(Hn)\) variables to bound the number of arbitrary Boolean functions and gives an almost-all-functions lower bound in **head count**. Their §4 compactness concerns the embedding dimension needed to realize a cross-Gram matrix with finite joint symbol–position data. Appendix A.1 explicitly shows value width has no effect for their affine scalar readout, because the output projection is linear. Their exact parity hierarchy is an \(H\)–length result for that readout. [Rajaraman–Sundaram–Tesfaye, v1 §§4–5 and A.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.04046v1)
- **Viswanathan, Dimitrov, Rocca, and Sirikonda**, *Formally Verified Autoresearch for Analyzing the Expressivity of Multi-Head Attention*, posted 18 October 2026. Their §3 normal form and threshold-degree bound concern an affine or linear threshold readout. Their Theorem 5 identifies symmetric head complexity with the number of weight-level changes while allowing shared additive positional embeddings. Appendix C explicitly compares this with Hsu–Xu at \(D=1\). Their Lean results about head count and strict nonsymmetric separation do not address value width when \(D>1\). [Viswanathan et al., v1 Theorem 5 and Appendix C](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.25341v1#A3)
- **Hsu**, *Lower bounds for one-layer transformers that compute parity*, posted 12 May 2026. This earlier preprint proves a head–readout-degree product bound for parity even with rational readout and position-specific parameters. Its quantitative obstruction does not depend on the value width. [Hsu, arXiv:2605.12171](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.12171)
- **Dooley, Yang, Zhu, Chiang, Cholak, and Pillay**, *VC Dimension and Expressivity of Real-Valued Transformers*, posted 25 September 2026. They derive VC dimension bounds in a multilayer softmax architecture. Proposition D.2 for arbitrary symmetric binary targets uses a target-specific positional encoding. Their symmetric inexpressivity result uses an alphabet of six symbols and a different fixed-architecture regime. Both rely on distinctions relevant to this question without giving a width obstruction in the position-free binary one-layer model. [Dooley et al., v1 §3 and Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.31999v1)
- **Opazo**, *Three Tokens Force Exponential Feature Rank in Nonnegative Kernel Attention*, arXiv:2608.11427. That paper cites Hsu–Xu and studies the rank of a nonnegative feature map defining the attention kernel. Its polynomial-readout results vary input length and feature rank and allow independent tokenwise values. The feature rank is the dimension of key/query kernel factors, a different resource from the value dimension after summing fixed-query softmax heads. [Opazo, arXiv:2608.11427](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11427)
- **Kileel, Trager, and Bruna**, *On the Expressive Power of Deep Polynomial Neural Networks*, NeurIPS 2019. They use algebraic images of coefficient maps and their dimension to study width in networks with coordinatewise power activations. This is methodological prior art for the algebraic dependence argument. The network map and the target coefficient-variety problem differ from sign-representing discrete Hamming profiles with a rational-softmax embedding. [Kileel–Trager–Bruna, NeurIPS primary paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper/2019/file/a0dc078ca0d99b5ebb465a9f1cad54ba-Paper.pdf)

The classical facts about sign patterns and symmetric threshold degree are prior as well. The search for author/title citations and equivalent value/feature-width statements also identified Kozachinskiy's VC work on infinite-precision transformers and the classical maximal-rank theorem for general rational projective embeddings. These do not by themselves turn into a lower bound on Hsu–Xu value width. Search terms included the full Hsu–Xu title, arXiv ID, author names, value dimension, polynomial readout, symmetric function, Warren, and maximal rank. The Semantic Scholar citation API could not be retrieved in this check. [Warren 1968](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1968-0226281-1), [Aspnes–Beigel–Furst–Rudich](https://cs.yale.edu/homes/aspnes/papers/stoc91voting-abstract.html), [Kozachinskiy](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.20195), [Ballico–Ellia](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1986__60_1_85_0.pdf)

## Joint-order upper directly from the source's additive basis

Write \(u=D/r\) and \(C_j=\binom{D+j}{j}\). For \(H=1\), identical attention scores and values 0 and 1 expose the normalized Hamming weight in one coordinate, and \(r=1\). For \(H\ge2\), the Hsu–Xu factorization construction already gives \(d_*\le D\). If \(D<49r\), that is enough for the order comparison. Otherwise choose \(p=2r\) and \(b=\lceil H^{1/p}\rceil\). The standard binomial estimate gives

\[
 H^{1/r}\le C_r^{1/r}\le e(1+u)<3(1+u).
\]

For \(u\ge49\), \(u^2\ge48(u+1)\), so

\[
 2p(b-1)\le 4r\sqrt{3(1+u)}\le D.
\]

Hsu–Xu Lemma 14 therefore supplies a \(D\)-basis of \([0,HD]\) using at most \(2p+1\) elements of \([H]\). Lemma 13 adds a constant coordinate and encodes each univariate polynomial of degree at most \(HD\) with a homogeneous readout of degree \(D\). Lemma 11 implements the chosen polynomials divided by a common positive denominator. Value dimension is at most \(2p+2\le 6r\). Together both cases give \(d_*\le 49\min(D,r)\). This bound is an explicit consequence of the published construction and requires no maximal-rank theorem. [Hsu–Xu, v1 §5.3–§5.5, Lemmas 11–14](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04243#S5)

## Lower by algebraic dependence and roots

Set \(T=HD\) and \(m=H+d(H+1)+C_d\). After scaling the Hamming variable to \(x=|\mathbf x|/n\) in \([0,1]\), the summed value coordinates are \(P_i(x)/Q(x)\) with all numerator and denominator degrees at most \(H\) and \(Q(0)=1\). The original denominator has positive values throughout \([0,1]\). Allow every compatible tuple of degree-\(H\) polynomials with these properties. The \(H\) nonconstant coefficients of \(Q\), \(d(H+1)\) coefficients of the \(P_i\), and \(C_d\) coefficients of a degree-at-most-\(D\) readout \(g\) are the \(m\) parameters \(\theta\). The coefficient functions \(a_0(\theta),\dots,a_T(\theta)\) of the cleared numerator \(Q^D g(P_1/Q,\ldots,P_d/Q)\) have degree at most \(q=D+1\) in \(\theta\), independent of \(n\). Zero margins at the sampled weights can be made strict by a small signed shift of the constant readout coefficient.

Suppose \(m<T\). Choose \(E\) large enough that \(\binom{T+E}{T}>\binom{m+qE}{m}\). For every multiindex \(\alpha\) of length \(T\) and sum at most \(E\), consider the parameter polynomial \(a_T^{E-|\alpha|}\prod_{i<T} a_i^{\alpha_i}\). All have degree at most \(qE\). Their number exceeds the dimension of that polynomial space, so a linear dependence gives a nonzero polynomial \(F\) with

\[
 F(a_0/a_T,\ldots,a_{T-1}/a_T)=0
\]

for every model numerator of degree \(T\). The monomials in the new coefficient variables are distinct, and the dependence coefficients are not all zero, so \(F\) is nonzero. This is the elementary algebraic-dependence implication of the parameter count.

Pick a monic degree-\(T\) polynomial \(P\) with \(T\) simple roots in \((0,1)\) and monic coefficient vector outside \(F=0\). The required coefficient set is nonempty because the simple-interior-root locus contains an open ball, witnessed by opposite signs at the ends of disjoint root intervals and the intermediate value theorem. A nonzero polynomial cannot vanish on an open ball. By continuity, the roots of \(P\) can also be chosen rational and distinct. At each sufficiently large \(n\), put one symmetric sign change in the grid gap containing each root of \(P\), choosing either adjacent gap if the root is a grid point. Fix one initial sign. The resulting \(n\)-bit profile has threshold degree exactly \(T\). Every strict representing numerator of degree at most \(T\) must have exactly one root in each selected gap, by the intermediate value theorem and the degree bound. After monic normalization its coefficient vector is therefore arbitrarily close to that of \(P\) as \(n\) increases, since the observed root positions are within \(1/n\) of their targets and coefficients are elementary symmetric polynomials. For all sufficiently large \(n\), every such vector lies in the open complement of \(F=0\), contradicting the coefficient relation. This proves \(m\ge T\) for universal width without invoking a counting theorem for signs.

If \(H\ge3\) and \(d\le(D-2)/2\), this necessary bound gives \(C_d\ge DH/2-d\). Also \(C_{d+1}/C_d\ge3\), hence \(C_{d+1}\ge HD+1\) since \(DH/2\ge 3d+1\). Thus \(r\le d+1\le2d\). In the complementary range \(D\le2d+1\le3d\). Every universal width is positive, and if \(H=1,2\), \(r\le H\) follows directly from the binomial coefficients. Thus \(d_*\ge \min(D,r)/3\). In the concrete regime \(D\ge3,H\ge4^D,d\le D-2\), \(C_d\le\binom{2D}{D}\le4^D/2\le H/2\), giving \(m\le(D-1)H+D-2+H/2<DH\), as claimed.

The new candidate contribution is the dimension lower bound for this specified attention class and its combination with the sharper upper consequence. The two lower arguments use standard ingredients, and the algebraic proof above is a manuscript-level argument with no kernel certificate attached to this audit. A novelty claim for the application requires the same qualification as any targeted priority search.
