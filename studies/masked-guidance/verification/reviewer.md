# Mathematical significance of the masked-guidance manuscript

Read-only review of `studies/masked-guidance/paper.tex`, SHA-256 `10e2461b754dfade0b1d677cc2f0498e8c1d93ec9fff7d3b01e6c3ddb0539efc`.

The two-bit counterexample matches the source's explicitly defined R3/R4 comparison. Each region is a singleton, the two classes have equal positive priors, all target one-coordinate projections are shared, and the target has a private endpoint of positive mass. As \(w\) increases, the named sampler moves mass monotonically to the shared endpoint. Its strictly positive limiting mass contradicts the source's limiting-support claim as well as its ordering conjecture. The source Proposition 3.3(1) concerning target support is compatible with the result. [ICLR source and supplement](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/hash/a14193e9d9fb0b03af0b717de1cac8ac-Abstract-Conference.html).

The quantitative product-target theorem shows why the counterexample is more than a particular small support. For any \(\varepsilon>0\), rare shared probability below \(\varepsilon\) becomes arbitrarily close to one under guidance at sufficiently large finite \(w\). The rates explain this through a large **marginal** class posterior at an early reveal, even though the final state must then fall in shared support. The two-coordinate ternary example also makes both class supports proper and incomparable within the full unmasked alphabet, with the entire mixture of full support. Consequently neither a single unshared token nor an omitted mixture label explains the effect.

The path theorem expresses the exact endpoint law as a finite sum over reveal orders and identifies the rate and prefactor from the minimum accumulated posterior gaps. Standard finite-sum asymptotics apply once the clock has been converted into the correct successor race. The new content is this model-specific endpoint statement and its contrast with ordering by terminal privacy. An independent rerun of the exact Python experiment passed. The current theorem concerns exact conditional rates in the single-token continuous-time model. Its mathematical significance does not depend on extrapolation to trained networks, parallel decoding, or heuristic logit blending.
