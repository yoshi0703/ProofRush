# Formal and computational verification

The formal negations are expressed as necessary instances of the human-authored universal claims. Both are over two arbitrary binary class distributions with equal priors. Restricting a universal assertion to a valid subfamily is logically sufficient for a counterexample. The witness distributions have nonnegative rational entries, sum to one, have proper supports, overlap in both coordinates and have a private target word. The independent branch and time calculations make explicit why the formal terminal probabilities match the source generator. [Original statement and model, pages 2–7 and 24](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2026/file/a14193e9d9fb0b03af0b717de1cac8ac-Paper-Conference.pdf)

The exact rational program additionally computes both six-decimal terminal intervals, tests the two-coordinate ternary product variant and evaluates the paper's two-dimensional formula independently. The written theorem supplies the uniform finite-strength lower bound; the Lean finite-strength inequality in the product example is the explicit 99% bound for the three-bit witness. The general proof for the diagonal family in all dimensions and the ternary strengthening are in the paper. The physical-clock derivation uses the usual exponential waiting-time description of a finite continuous-time Markov chain. The Lean modules formalize the score identities, time change, holding-time integral and composition of jumps. A path-space construction of CTMC probability measures is outside their scope.
