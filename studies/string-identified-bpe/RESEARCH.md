Their 23 November 2026 preprint gives the answer under unique decodability and poses the extension in Sections 8 and 11. Appendix G reports small exhaustive searches up to three merges. ## Evidence for admission

**Correctness.** The single word disproves the universal quantification over tie choices at budget four. The proof uses the actual spelling as the identity at every step. It proves that every pair of spellings has count no greater than the selected pair and that a full comparator with gain ten is permitted by the partial-rule optimum. The source authors' exact optimizer and our separately written exhaustive code both find optimum ten. Independent kernel replay checks the formal negation and includes only Lean's usual foundational axioms. [Verification register](VERIFY.md)

**Originality.** Searches using the source identifier, title, authors, spelling-identity model, failure of unique decodability, approximation guarantee and witness ratio located the source's earlier counterexamples to different assertions. The authors had shown a failure of their shadow inequality, and the separation of fresh and string-identified optima. Their own data report the approximation inequality surviving their tested non-unique instances. Neither the finite-budget violation nor the large-budget construction was located in the primary literature consulted. Unindexed work can easily escape such a search. [Source Sections 8 and 11, Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08671v3) · [Reproducible source comparison](verification/source-review.md)

**Nontriviality.** The earlier example's comparator saves three against an input whose best initial rule saves one. That observation does not prove failure of the approximation ratio. Our input supports two complete greedy histories saving four and ten. In the general construction, an existing atom at each dyadic scale allows the comparator to collect an additional occurrence after every step. The six left/right phases keep all initial pair counts uniformly bounded relative to the number of seed scales. Higher-weight private rows then make every greedy tie resolution take the wrong rules.

**Significance at v3.0.0.** The finite counterexample answered a stated extension of a mathematical model of tokenizer training. The first family put an upper bound of one sixth on the best-tie worst-case limit in that model. It identifies pre-existing ambiguous spellings as a source of lost approximation performance distinct from raw overlapping pair counts. It is one answer to one human-posed question. The fixed-budget construction, the general family and the numerical examples are parts of that answer. The result gives no measurement for trained language models or deployed tokenizers.

## Assumptions challenged

- A word in the witness consists of fourteen specified tokens. Its underlying spelling has length 64. Beginning the algorithm on 64 separate character tokens gives a different input. The paper's own Appendix D uses compound atoms inside rows, including two with different terminal letters. Its released checker takes arbitrary sequences of string-valued atoms. A separate restriction to one common marker confined to row-final initial tokens has yet to be settled by this study.
- Equal-symbol runs of length three give one full replacement. Checking overlapping adjacent frequencies would alter which greedy choices are legal.
- The compact witness depends on a legal adverse tie choice. The four-budget family with private rows forces the violation under every order, and the large-budget construction also bounds the best possible tie order.
- The comparator does not need to be proved optimal to refute the guarantee. The complete partial-optimizer computation provides an extra check on the finite value.
- The two independent family checkers originally disagreed about how to increment different-pair counts next to a run of equal symbols. The first version of the secondary checker skipped such a boundary. It was repaired and retested on the word with lengths 1, 1, 2. All 465 parameter checks still passed. The finite witness, primary checker, and proof were unchanged.

## Matching the rate and identifying the role of vocabulary size

During 20:34–21:03 UTC on 26 November, the research team investigated how quickly the worst ratio can decrease and whether a fixed initial vocabulary changes its limit. The earlier release supplied the upper construction. First attempts at a universal lower bound charged a merge node to an original cherry and gave \(1/O(k^2)\), then \(1/O(k)\). The missing information was time along a chain of singleton attachments. On each side, successive leaves are original neighbors. A short time gap gives one of only \(2ks\) labels, and each chain can have at most \(2k/s\) long gaps. Choosing \(s\) near \(\sqrt{k}\) matches the earlier construction. The proof records literal positions and works even when many different merge trees spell the same token. [Human and AI roles](#question-dates-and-attribution) · [Forest proof with an independent accounting](verification/forest-review.md) · [Final all-input theorem](lean/UniversalRate.lean)

A separate count showed that the unbounded-vocabulary result cannot be attained as \(k\) grows if the number of distinct initial spellings stays bounded. The numerical bound for fixed character alphabets is prior work. Here, two surviving boundary blocks determine their visible pair by isolated replay on their initial atom sequence. Summing a short-pair inequality at every time gives the fixed-vocabulary extension without assuming that the maximum raw frequency is monotone. The main formal archive includes the exact finite inequality and uniform approximation over all words with a fixed type cap.
