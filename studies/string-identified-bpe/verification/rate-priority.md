# Focused prior-art and priority audit: ambiguous-initial-spelling BPE

Checked 27 November 2026 (JST), against publicly accessible primary papers and arXiv version pages. Conclusion: **the unrestricted-alphabet Θ(k^(-1/2)) utility ratio for literal-spelling collisions appears to be a distinct result in this search, but novelty and priority are not established by a literature search.** The fixed-initial-vocabulary estimate is **not a new numerical inequality in the standard fresh-symbol model**: the same finite expression was already published in Theorem 5(b) of Baliga–Xie–Byju's companion v3. Extending that expression to arbitrary initially ambiguous token spellings, using a different argument at the point where their proof uses fresh-symbol monotonicity, is the accurately scoped possible contribution.

## Model and quantifiers

The model audited here has an initial *sequence of nonempty token spellings*, possibly including distinct spellings a, aa, etc.; tokens are identified by their literal string, merging x,y outputs xy, and a collision with an already present token is identified. An occurrence is a position in the initial token sequence, even when its spelling equals another occurrence. Greedy always full-merges a current pair maximizing either the actual replacement count or the raw overlapping count; compare with ≤k partial merges; all greedy ties are included. Ratio means greedy **saving divided by optimum saving**, when optimum is positive, not final compressed length ratio. A string or table/corpus and the size of its initial token alphabet must be identified separately in every theorem.

The local unrestricted lower proof uses occurrence-merge forests, decomposes unary attachment chains by side, and assigns a short chronological gap to an initial adjacent-pair label (side, later rule, gap). No comparable bound was located in the sources below. The lower proof does not rely on unique decoding of the initial spellings. Existing matching O(k^(-1/2)) upper constructions use a vocabulary growing with k; therefore they do **not** imply a vanishing ratio when the number A of distinct initial spelling types is fixed.

## Closest sources, with scope

1. **Baliga, Xie and Byju, arXiv:2611.08671v3**, initially 5 November, v3 23 November 2026: [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08671v3), [full text, especially Appendix D](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.08671v3). Their principal tight constant is for atomic/fresh-symbol inputs. Appendix D §“The model” identifies tokens with spellings, states correspondence for uniquely decodable initial inputs, and explicitly asks whether its Theorem 13 survives when that condition fails. It gives examples with initial atoms a,b,ab showing the proof's shadow inequality fails without unique decoding. Those examples and that question are the closest source for the collision variant, and substantially limit any claim that the model itself is new. Appendix D also describes practical atoms as characters, some with word markers; an arbitrary initial vocabulary of compound spellings is a broader mathematical model and should be labelled accordingly. The paper does not state the Θ(k^(-1/2)) collision rate in the inspected version. An answer to a stronger mathematical extension should not be described as a result for every deployed byte- or character-initialized trainer.

2. **Baliga, Xie and Byju, arXiv:2611.07346v3**, initially 5 November, v3 23 November 2026: [version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.07346v3), [Theorem 5(b), §6 and Appendix G.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.07346v3). This is the decisive fixed-alphabet prior art. Theorem 5(b) already proves, for atomic/fresh-symbol BPE under both greedy rankings, `P−G ≤ G(2/(M−1)+2 N_M/k)`, `N_M=∑_{j=2}^M A^j`, and fixed-A convergence of G/OPT to 1. It additionally proves a **matching Θ(1/log k) deficit** on binary corpora (Theorem 6), and on a binary single string (Theorem 7), for A≥2. Thus the bounded-A limit, logarithmic order, and exact same displayed coefficients are established in the ordinary atomic model; only their extension to collision-identified *ambiguous initial spellings* is at issue. The standard binary counterexamples belong to the broad class of inputs with ≤A initial spellings, so they supply the fixed-A worst-case lower obstruction there. They do not automatically supply a lower obstruction for a class that requires every input itself to be non-uniquely decodable.

   Their Appendix G.2 uses two specific facts: same original atomic substring forces the same current two token types, and maximum raw pair frequency is nonincreasing because a fresh output type is introduced. Under arbitrary literal-spelling collisions the latter monotonicity is false; source 1 Appendix D exhibits an increase even after a full merge. The new local fixed-A argument instead bounds short-pair mass **at each stage** by that stage's greedy gain and sums over stages. Its result has the same expression, but a fresh-type proof cannot simply be cited for the collision setting. Related isolation and short-pair counting ideas are plainly prior; describe the work as a model extension, not discovery of fixed-alphabet asymptotic optimality or a new formula.

3. **Kozma and Voderholzer, Theoretical Analysis of Byte-Pair Encoding**, arXiv v1 13 November 2024, [v2 30 October 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08671v2), [ESA 2026 published text](https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol388-esa2026/html/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.80/LIPIcs.ESA.2026.80.html). The paper's §2 explicitly requires every output symbol to be new so reverse substitution is unambiguous, and establishes a 1/3 replacement-greedy bound against partial-merge OPE; v2 and subsequent work improve/complete the standard-model asymptotics. Its pair-packing/shadow charging is closely related in spirit, but it assumes a different symbol identity. Its approximation results are not evidence of a constant approximation in the collision model, and its fixed-alphabet question concerns atomic alphabets.

4. **Zouhar et al., A Formal Perspective on Byte-Pair Encoding**, Findings of ACL 2023, [paper and bibliographic record](https://aclanthology.org/2023.findings-acl.38/). Their stated guarantee depends on the optimum's backward curvature, not an unconditional k-only bound; empirical ≈0.37 should not be repeated as a universal guarantee. It is important optimization and string-identified-token prior art, but the retrieved source does not state the arbitrary-ambiguous-initial-spelling Θ(k^(-1/2)) result.

5. **Baliga, Xie and Byju, arXiv:2611.13568v1**, 10 November 2026, [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13568v1). This connects merge histories and grammar trees and proves hardness for the **remaining compressed length**, including binary strings and unary datasets. Its occurrence-tree/grammar bridge is directly relevant structural prior art, although its stated objective is not the greedy-saving/partial-saving ratio and its merges introduce fresh types. A merge-tree representation itself is not novel.

6. **Bastan, He, Pavan and Vinodchandran, arXiv:2611.15074v1**, 11 November 2026, [record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15074). The square root in its O(√M log n) result is a bicriteria factor involving maximum document length M, a vocabulary cardinality budget, and final token count; it is not a bound in merge budget k for greedy utility and does not imply the result here. This is an important false-positive in keyword searches.

7. **Baliga, Xie and Byju, GreedTok companion**, same item 2: its graph/vertex-coverage reductions concern direct vocabulary selection, a different algorithm from BPE's stepwise pair merge. It is relevant to graph-based tokenization, but its 0.3287 GreedTok ratio and fixed-alphabet direct-token statements cannot be transplanted to this model without a reduction. It cites the primary works of Ion et al. for EPTAS/graph-based direct-token approximation, whose guarantees also use a distinct optimization problem.

## Search coverage and limits

Searches included the literal paper identifiers and titles; “byte pair encoding approximation arbitrary initial symbols concatenation collision”; “non-uniquely decodable”; “pair merge string identity approximation”; “greedy pair merges 1/sqrt compression”; “occurrence forest short chronological gaps”; fixed alphabets, pair packing and graph-based tokenization. Inspected linked official ACL Anthology, ESA/Dagstuhl and version-pinned arXiv primary sources, including the later related papers above. Searches are indexed-web searches, not exhaustive full-text coverage of all preprints, unpublished work, citations, code, patents, or adjacent grammar-compression literature. The latest revision page for arXiv:2611.08671 was separately checked, but indexing and releases can lag. Consequently the appropriate claim is “not found in this focused search; independent broader review needed,” not a definitive first result.
