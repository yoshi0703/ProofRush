# Priority review for catalogue item 5ed263d5

The claim under review is the explicit refutation of Pastorek's regular-graph inequality. The graph is the previously published graph G₁₄. Its extremal diameter and the general construction are credited to Brand, Guiduli and Imrich. Their paper describes the graph and determines its diameter. A partition into six cliques immediately turns it into a counterexample to the later catalogue question. The argument is elementary and its subject is a machine-generated conjecture. The publication status of this note is an unreviewed research report. ## What the original source asserts

The author's merged data identify the formula by ID and mark it proposed. Its formal statement and proof fields are null. Nontriviality means at least two vertices. Regularity is the existence of a common degree. The paper defines the vertex clique cover number as the chromatic number of the complementary graph. The evaluation wrapper treats disconnected diameter as undefined. The two Lean theorems refute both the printed catalogue statement and the exact connected evaluation domain. The source imposes no restriction excluding bridges. ## Correctness and mathematical significance

The negative answer is an elementary corollary of known diameter results. In Brand, Guiduli and Imrich's G₄₂ the diameter is 29. A diameter path gives 15 disjoint edges on its 30 vertices. Together with the other 12 vertices as singletons these form a clique partition of size 27. Thus the proposed bound would give 29 ≤ 28. The priority claim concerns the explicit resolution of the 2026 candidate and the accompanying formal certificate. [Earlier extremal family](https://people.cs.uchicago.edu/~laci/students/guiduli.dir/barry-brand-imrich.pdf)

The statement was transcribed in a file separate from the witness. Its conditions were checked independently against the paper, catalogue and evaluation rules. Two verification kernels checked the exported negations. A separately written Python calculation checked the exact diameter and clique cover number by different algorithms. The proof's contribution is a small explicit false positive for a graph-conjecturing dataset and a reproducible formal refutation. A human referee has not reviewed this manuscript.
